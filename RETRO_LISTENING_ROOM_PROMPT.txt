Use case: ui-mockup
Asset type: preview-only product design concept image for a music web app, no implementation.
Primary request: A sophisticated immersive retro DJ listening booth web interface that lets everyday music makers explore a song's separate instrument and vocal layers, then record their own. The user explicitly wants 3D with instruments depicting actual real world counterparts and sound visualizations. Replace abstract sound sculptures with recognizable real instruments and recorded-layer tape objects. One spacious landscape desktop interface concept screenshot, NOT a marketing poster, no multiple panels.
Scene: fixed gently elevated frontal view into a cozy intimate late-evening studio corner. Walnut DJ desk in foreground, a pair of believable vintage silver turntables at far left and far right and a SMALL simple central analog mixer, not a sprawling wall of equipment. Guitar on stand at left background, recognizable compact electric keyboard at right background, a warm vintage microphone at center back, small snare and brushed cymbal in the right rear. Realistic spatial proportions, desk below middle so instruments visible. Everything comfortably visible from one viewpoint without movement. Dusk blue room, warm cream labels, restrained amber indicator lights, tactile wood grain, brushed aluminum, worn paper labels, analog VU meters. Melancholic, welcoming, personal, calm, no nightclub neon, no lasers, no people.
Layer interaction: Four large, tidy interactive cassette-tape tiles in a clearly readable single row on a low rack just above the front edge of the desk, styled as real vintage cassettes with cream paper labels. Exact labels: "Guitar", "Lead voice", "Harmony", "Rhythm". A small realistic line drawing of the corresponding guitar, microphone, microphone, drum is printed on each tape. The two vocal tapes visibly share the same microphone symbol, distinct written take names. Guitar and Lead voice tapes are warmly lit and show a tiny legible "Playing" indicator; Harmony and Rhythm are darker but legibly labeled "Off". Each cassette has a clear small play toggle under it; do not rely on color alone. Physical instrument props establish source identity; cassettes represent independent recorded layers.
Sound visualization: restrained thin amber oscilloscope-style waveform displayed on a narrow strip immediately above the Guitar cassette, and a different pale cream waveform above Lead voice. Muted tapes have a flat line. Near the small mixer there are two small real analog VU meters. Visual traces follow a consistent left-to-right direction and stay attached to their associated tapes; no giant floating holograms, no unrelated decorative particles, no interference with labels.
UI text: upper left small "Listening room"; upper right a visible understated action "Record my idea". Bottom left discrete "Pause" and "Volume" controls. Bottom center one gentle readable instruction: "Try the harmony." The image should communicate clickable musical exploration with almost no prose. All typography readable, restrained elegant utilitarian sans serif for controls and handwritten-looking only on cassette labels if still highly readable. No navigation menus, no feature cards, no login modal, no invented app brand, no slogans. Keep the number of physical controls low so this feels inviting to a returning casual musician, not a professional audio console. Strong atmospheric yet usable premium product concept.
